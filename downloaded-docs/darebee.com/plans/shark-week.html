Overview

Goal: Symptom relief + staying on track
Duration: 4-7 days, once a month
Structure: Intensity rises as you feel better

SHARK WEEK is a mini training plan for anyone who menstruates. It is designed to be repeated every cycle: Day 1 is always the first day of your period, and each day after that matches what your body is typically ready for. It starts gentle when symptoms are at their worst and builds back to full intensity by Day 7, so you finish the week strong instead of feeling like you lost one.

The science, briefly: you may have heard you should not train on your period. The evidence says otherwise. The first days of your cycle are a low-hormone phase in which strength training is well tolerated, and regular exercise measurably reduces cramps and period pain rather than making them worse. The catch is that a period is not one uniform week. Cramps and fatigue usually peak on days 1-2, then energy climbs steadily as estrogen rises, and by days 5-7 many people feel stronger than usual. This plan follows that curve.

What to Expect:

✓ Relief First: Gentle movement on the hardest days, chosen specifically to ease cramps.
✓ A Gradual Ramp: Intensity returns only as your body does.
✓ No Jumping Early On: Low-impact options while you are still tender and bloated.
✓ A Strong Finish: The plan ends at full intensity, not on the couch.
✓ Zero Guilt Built In: Every day can be swapped down. Showing up is the win.

Who is This For?

- Anyone who menstruates and wants a reliable monthly plan;
- Anyone whose regular training falls apart for a week every month;
- Anyone dealing with cramps, low energy, and the "should I even work out?" question;
- Anyone whose period is shorter than 7 days: do Days 1-3, then rejoin your regular plan.

Instructions

  1. Start the plan on the first day of your period. Day 1 = Day 1, every cycle.
  2. Days 1-4 use specific low-intensity workouts. Days 5-7 use the database filter: set the difficulty level (1 to 5) to match your fitness level and pick a workout.
  3. If your period ends early, finish the plan anyway as a ramp back into regular training, or rejoin your usual plan after Day 3. Both are correct.
  4. If symptoms run heavier than usual on any day, repeat an earlier day instead. The plan works around you, not the other way around.

Day 1

Focus: Relief. This is not a training day, it is a feel-better day.

What To Do:
✓ Shark Week Workout
✓ Optional: a 20-30 minute easy walk, or gentle marching in place at home

Why? Cramps are caused by prostaglandins making the uterus contract, and they peak in the first day or two. Gentle movement increases pelvic blood flow, which directly eases cramping, and the stretching holds target the lower back and hips where period pain likes to settle. Doing nothing usually feels worse than doing a little.

Day 2

Focus: Gentle movement. Still symptomatic for most people, so we keep it kind.

What To Do:
✓ Gentle Yoga or De-Stress Stretching
✓ Optional: easy steps, same as Day 1

Why? Yoga and stretching are among the best-studied interventions for period pain, and they work: slow holds relax the muscles that tense up around cramps and lower back ache. Nothing here loads a bloated or tender midsection.

Day 3

Focus: Reawakening. Symptoms are easing and estrogen is starting to climb.

What To Do:
✓ Cardio Light or Lazy Cardio
✓ Evening option: Stretch & Release

Why? Steady, low-impact cardio boosts circulation and mood without asking too much. No jumping yet: many people still feel heaviness and tenderness on Day 3, so everything stays on the ground.

Day 4

Focus: Building back. Real work returns, impact does not.

What To Do:
✓ Low Impact or Cardio Party

Why? By Day 4 most people can train properly again. Full body strength work with no jumping lets you push without pounding, and it restarts the adaptation response so you lose nothing this week.

Day 5

Focus: Strength. You are back.

What To Do:
✓ Upper Body Strength Workout or Full Body Strength Workout. If you own dumbbells, select workouts with equipment "Dumbbells".

Why? Rising estrogen has a mildly anabolic effect: this is genuinely a good time to strength train, not just an acceptable one. Pick your normal difficulty level.

Day 6

Focus: Power returns. Energy and pain tolerance are measurably improving.

What To Do:
✓ HIIT Workout or Metabolic Conditioning Workout

Why? High intensity work is back on the menu. If you are still symptomatic, swap in Day 3 or Day 4 instead: the plan adjusts to your cycle, not the other way around.

Day 7

Focus: Full send. Finish the week strong.

What To Do:
✓ Any workout you want, at your normal difficulty.

Why? Late follicular energy is real: many people set personal bests in this window. Ending the plan at full intensity means your period week closes on strength, not on survival. See you next month.
