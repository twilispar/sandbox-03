The Workout Manual

Read once. Use forever.

DAREBEE workouts are designed for all fitness levels and are easy to navigate. This manual covers everything: how to read a poster, how to count reps, what the difficulty levels mean, and how rest time works.

Before any workout, warm up first - it only takes a few minutes and it's the cheapest injury insurance there is. Here's how →
Workout categories

Cardio

Boost cardiovascular endurance, burn calories, and improve stamina. Ideal for heart health and fat burning.

Strength

Build muscle and improve muscle definition. Focus on resistance and compound movements.

Metcon

Metabolic Conditioning - combines strength and cardio at high intensity to maximize energy output and endurance.

HIIT

Short bursts of intense activity with recovery periods. Efficient for fat burning and boosting metabolism.

Combat

Builds strength, agility, and speed using martial arts-inspired techniques and movement patterns.

Stretching

Improve flexibility and mobility. Good for recovery, easing tension, and improving range of motion.

Yoga

Mindful sessions for flexibility, balance, and relaxation while promoting mental clarity.

Wellness

Gentle routines for overall health, recovery, and well-being. Good for active rest days or easing into fitness.

Difficulty levels

Use the difficulty filter at the top of the workouts, programs, and challenges pages to find content suited to your fitness level. The easiest way to pick your level: look at the exercises listed for each and ask which ones you can comfortably do today.

Level 1 · Light Standing and seated exercises for beginners or those with limited mobility. Ideal for active recovery days.
You'll see: walking, seated exercises, standing-only movements.
Level 2 · Easy Low-impact exercises - floor work is fine but no planks. Suitable for those recovering from injury or with joint issues.
You'll see: squat holds, reverse lunges, butt kicks, hops.
Level 3 · Normal A balance of challenge and effectiveness without high-impact moves. Works for most fitness levels.
You'll see: jumping jacks, forward and side lunges, squats, high knees, planks, wall push-ups, tricep dips, get-ups.
Level 4 · Hard High-impact exercises for those ready for more intensity.
You'll see: push-ups, jumping lunges, jump squats, squat hops, all burpees, up & down planks, plank jacks, plank jump-ins.
Level 5 · Advanced Complex calisthenics for individuals with solid fitness experience.
You'll see: all pull-ups, jump knee-tucks, hook kicks, and other advanced compound moves.
The 1-5 difficulty levels (light, easy, normal, hard and advance) on the site describe how demanding the exercises themselves are = aka workout complexity.

The Levels I-III on a poster are intensity options within that workout - you pick how many sets to do, and you can move up over time. Only circuit type workouts have this allowing you to scale them based on how tired you are or how much time you have on the day.
How to read a workout poster

Each workout poster is read left to right and contains four things:

Exercises

Illustrated steps with movement images

Reps

Number of repetitions per exercise

Sets

Total number of sets to complete

Rest

How long to rest between sets

If an exercise has two images (e.g., jumping jacks, squats) it's a dynamic movement. A single image (e.g., plank, wall-sit) means a static hold. We also have a video library for most exercises.
Types of workouts

Circuit Workouts

Complete all exercises in the circuit before resting. The number of sets is listed at the top of the poster. Repeat the full circuit for your chosen level.

Level I - 3 sets (~10 min) Level II - 5 sets (~15-20 min) Level III - 7 sets (~25-30 min)


Choose circuits when you want conditioning with your strength work. Moving from exercise to exercise without rest keeps your heart rate up, so you build muscle and stamina at the same time - and Levels I-III let you scale the session to the time and energy you have that day.

Browse all circuit strength workouts →

Classic Workouts

Complete all sets for each exercise before moving to the next. Sets are specified under each individual exercise on the poster.

Choose classic when you want focused strength work. Resting between sets lets you recover and give every rep your full effort, which is better for building strength and muscle definition - and it lets you work at your own pace, one exercise at a time.

Browse all classic strength workouts →

Not sure which to pick? Start with circuits - most DAREBEE strength workouts are circuits for a reason: they deliver the most in the least time. Mix in classic workouts when you want to slow down and concentrate on individual exercises.
Understanding reps

Counting reps

"20 climbers" means 20 total, counting both legs - that's 10 per leg.

Time-based exercises

Some exercises are measured in seconds instead of reps, e.g. "60 seconds plank". Hold or keep moving for the full duration. Any clock, phone timer, or stopwatch works.

10-count / 20-count

Hold the position and count to the number - no timer needed, which is handy mid-circuit. It's roughly a 10 or 20 second hold. Counting slower makes it harder, counting faster makes it easier.

Switching sides

Strength: switch halfway. Cardio: switch every rep. Combat: halfway for technique, every rep for speed.

To Failure / MAX

As many reps as possible while maintaining proper form. Stop when form breaks down.

Reps + (e.g. 5+)

Complete the minimum number of reps to advance to the next exercise. Do more if it feels too easy.

Make it Easier / Make it Harder

Many workouts include suggestions for scaling the difficulty down or up - swap a move, drop the reps, or add intensity. Use them to fit the workout to your current fitness level, not the other way around.

Combos

"3 combos: 10 high knees + 2 push-ups" - complete that pairing 3 times before moving to the next exercise.

Rest time

Circuit workouts: no rest between exercises - only rest after completing the full circuit before repeating it.

Classic workouts: reps and sets are listed under each exercise on the poster.

Rest time guidelines by level:

Level I Up to 2 minutes
Level II 60 seconds or less
Level III 30 seconds or less
